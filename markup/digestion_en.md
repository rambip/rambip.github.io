TL;DR: I have digestive problems. They greatly impact my life, so I explain them here.

**Disclaimer**: this page contains sensible health information. I trust you, as a reader, to treat them responsibly.

Some disabilities are visible, others are hidden. Hidden disabilities tend to crate confusion and tensions, because outsiders misinterpret the behavior of the handicapped person. This is the case in particular for social interactions and at work: a friend or a colleague can interpret a behavior as rude / depressed / dumb / irresponsible, because he lacks the context of the handicap.

It has been the case for me. For quite some time now, I suffer from digestive issues, and they are a major challenge in my life. This page aims at providing context on the subject.

They are 2 other reasons why I decided to write this document. First, I think it might help me to relieve some of the psychological pressure I associate with this condition. If more people know it and accept it's a real ~health~ problem, I can start treating it as such, without having to hide my symptoms. Second, I think my condition is more widespread than one might think. You may even know other people who suffer from similar problems, and if it's the case I hope what you will read here will be a good start to help them.

# Symptoms (they suck)

A doctor would say that I suffer from chronic upper-abdominal discomfort, severe bloating and slow transit.

Although it's true, it's not very helpful to understand what the experience looks like. And if you want to understand the problems I face, you really have to understand the experience.

1. **Abdominal discomfort**. Imagine the sensation when you eat too much too quickly. It's slightly painful, it can hinder some of your actions, and it's especially unpleasant. You can't really pinpoint where the pain is: it's a diffuse sensation of discomfort. My main symptom is exactly that, except that it can persist all day long (not just after meals), that it can be a concern to fall asleep, and that your body constantly tries to find a posture that causes less discomfort. This last one creates muscle tensions, and more broadly back pain.

2. **Severe bloating**. This is the cause of abdominal discomfort. My intestines hold more food, gas and liquid than the average person, which increases the pressure. My abdomen is not flat, but inflated.

3. **Slow transit**. Some days, I can't use the bathroom. Generally, food takes more time than average to traverse my digestive track

4. **Chronic**. The symptoms I described are not constant, fortunately, or at least not the worst version of them. But I have lived with it for a very long time, probably since 2023, 3 years ago. The symptoms appear in phases. I can feel ok for 2 weeks, then worse for 1 week, and suffer terribly for 3 days.


The symptoms I described impact my whole life. I would say that the first consequence is the difficulty to focus. My whole brain directs its attention to the discomfort, and I have almost no way to counter this. This makes working, learning, and even playing games harder. Then, there is overall mood. After a few days of discomfort and not being able to focus properly, my mood goes down rapidly. After this comes sleep, and then physical performances. I stayed slightly underweight for 3 years, not as a decision but as a consequence of my digestive issues.

I don't want you to feel sorry for me. I want to give the most detailed explanation, so that you understand the context.

# Solutions (and non-solutions)

After multiple years of symptoms, diet changes and tests, I have a better idea of what improve my symptoms, and what worsen them.

Let's start with the triggers.

## Negative impact

Clearly, the one thing that have the most impact on my digestive life is diet.

If some meals cause problems because of the macro-nutritional content (not enough fiber, too many carbs), much of the time crisis originates from specific foods.

Typically, these food triggers are onions, lentils, wheat in large quantities, lactose (milk) ...

Food I can't tolerate corresponds approximatively with the "[FODMAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FODMAP)" food list. These are aliment containing specific sugars such as Galactose or Fructans, that can't be digested in the intestines (nobody has the enzymes to break them down). I don't react automatically to these triggers. Some of the time I can tolerate them very well, and in crisis periods I react to small amount (around 12 - 24h later).

Stress can also be a trigger, but it's almost negligeable compared to food.

## Neutral impact

I have been advised to try a lot of different strategies to treat or at least cope with the problem. Most of them were useless, like:
- relaxation and meditation exercises (they can help me feel slightly better, but no impact on my transit)
- antispasmodics
- drinking more, or drinking less
- chewing more
- doing more sport (this can lead to a small improvement, but not as efficient as people could think)


## Positive impact

By far the most efficient solution is to control what ends up in my meals, and I must be quite strict about it.

I have found other tricks that can be useful, but the gains are minor.
- applying a heating pad on my abdomen
- extensive walking sessions

# Theories

As I said before, I still don't have a good theory of how my medical condition works. And this is extremely frustrating.

I have studied the problem with more or less competent medical professionals, and I still don't have a clear diagnostic. To be fair, there are still a lot of scientific uncertainty and unknowns about the intestines, so much so that physicians very often rely on an extremely broad and unspecific diagnosis:

The **irritable bowel syndrome**.

Since the treatments they have proposed me were ineffective, I kept investigating by myself. And little by little, I have ruled out some theories:

- It's not gluten intolerance or celiac disease. I have tested gluten-free diet without much effect and I tested negative for celiac in my blood tests.
- It's not genetics. I was great 3 years ago, and my family don't have such problems.
- It's probably not just intolerance. I have tested to exclude almost every aliment during weeks, and symptom s never resolve.
- It's not just a bad habit like "not enough sport", "not enough fiber" or "too much stress". I am very mindful about doing enough sport, eating a lot of vegetables and doing meditation / relaxation.
- It's not psychological. I clearly have food triggers, even when I feel perfect.


Below is my current best theory. It is based upon relatively new research (~10 to ~2 last years) and is not accepted at all by the French medical community. It can be completely wrong, but I would say I'm 20% confident this is exactly what I have, and 80% confident at least half of it is true.

> After a food-poisoning (vomiting, stomach flu), I have been exposed to some bacteria that produce a toxin called [Cdt-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytolethal_distending_toxin). My immune system reacted to it by creating antibodies. Unfortunately, a protein found in Human cell membranes called [Vinculin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinculin) has a very similar molecular profile. Some of the created antibodies can bind to and react with Vinculin, and a selection process causes this antibody to become predominant, despite **Cdt-B** having disappeared. This is an autoimmune reaction: my body reacts and attacks an internal element. This autoimmune reaction go on for months or even years. It does not have a lot of consequences in the rest of the body, but it destroys some cells in the intestines: cells of Cajal and a part of the Muenteric Plexus. As a consequence, my digestion machinery is impaired, which causes a very slow transit. Finally, this slow transit allows parasitic bacteria to settle in my small intestine, and consume the non-digestible sugars in the foods (the infamous FODMAPs found in lentils, cabbage and so on). They create gas (methane) and slow down my transit even more. This food poisoning my have taken place multiple times, with worse symptoms each time.

> This condition is referred to as [post-infectious IBS](https://aboutibs.org/what-is-ibs/post-infectious-ibs/).

# Next steps

Treatment is also underdeveloped in France.

If it does not work, I will try other medicines like prokinetics (they stimulate digestion) or specific diets like the "elemental diet".

It if still does not work, I will do a fibroscopy to have more insights.

If none of the previous idea work, I will consolidate my current diet, while checking for vitamin or nutriment defficiencies.
